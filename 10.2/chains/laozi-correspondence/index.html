RSM v10.2 — Laozi Correspondence & Guodian Visual Paleography

Manuscript Stratification, Structural Mappings (::), and the Chu Visual Dictionary

This document reads the Warring States Guodian bamboo slips ([G], c. 300 BCE), the Western Han Mawangdui silk manuscripts ([M], c. 168 BCE), and the received text ([R]) alongside the Recursive Structural Model (RSM).

Because 01_Structural_Chain.md, 02_Mathematical_Construction.md, and 04_Looking_at_a_Tree.md derive and illustrate the model independently without relying on any Chinese text, no character in this document supplies a hidden premise for the mathematics, and no equation forces a manuscript reading. 0. Witness Stratification & Methodological Guardrails

0.1 Reading Forward from the Bamboo ([G] ⟶\longrightarrow⟶ [M] ⟶\longrightarrow⟶ [R])

Mainstream scholarship routinely treats Guodian, Mawangdui, and the Received Daodejing as interchangeable witnesses of a single homogeneous book, normalizing Guodian’s geometric graphs 亡 (wáng) and 又 (yòu) into later standardized 無 (wú) and 有 (yǒu). Between the burial of the Guodian slips (c. 300 BCE) and the copying of the Mawangdui silk manuscripts (c. 168 BCE) stood the Qin imperial script standardization (Shuwen Tongzi):

  1. The Guodian Stratum ([G], Bundles A, B, C & Taiyi Shengshui) — Visual-Geometric Syntax: Chapters 1 and 42 are absent from the surviving Guodian bundles. The Warring States Chu script did not need Chapter 1’s subscript definitions because it carried the structural topology directly inside the brush-stroke architecture of the graphs (亡, 又, 行, 衜/道, 心, 非, 曰, 皆, 可/名/命, 返, 谷/浴/浧, 天坨/天拖).
  2. The Mawangdui ([M]) & Received ([R]) Stratum — Algebraic-Subscript Syntax: After Qin/Han standardization flattened regional Chu glyph variants into uniform boxy characters—severing the visual identity between 可 and 名 and wiping out the geometric diagrams inside 亡, 又, and 道—the compilers of the post-Guodian opening chapter (Chapter 1) executed a syntactic repair: they unpacked the lost graphic identity 名 = 可 into the explicit clause 名可名 and deployed 無名 (the cut absent, 0n=1n0_n = 1_n0n​=1n​) and 有名 (the cut present, 0n≠1n0_n \neq 1_n0n​=1n​) as explicit notation for the two relations, stating in words what the Chu script previously drew in ink. Both compounds are already on the slips (§3.3).

0.2 The Correspondence (::) & Phonophore Guardrails

  • No Register Substitution: Context_A :: Role == Context_B :: Role states a structural isomorphism of relational roles; it never claims that Warring States scribes wrote modern differential calculus or that a Chinese word is exhausted by a coordinate chart. Every major mapping below specifies its Shared Structure, Types, Non-Transferable Attributes, and Failure Condition.
  • The Chu-Engineer Admissible-Vocabulary Constraint & Phonophore Guardrail: Primary glosses must use concrete physical, hydraulic, craft, or somatic mechanics verifiable by a 300 BCE Chu engineer (e.g., Foundry bellows, river weirs, shoulder-poles, timber, clay), and phonetic loan elements (such as 工 in 江 or 每/母 in 海) must be respected as phonophores rather than over-read as semantic compounds in primary definitions.

2. The Three-Way Chu Glyph Distinction: 行 vs. Intersection 道 (衜) vs. Switchback 道

Across the Guodian slips (Laozi Jia, Yi, Bing, Taiyi Shengshui, Wuxing), three distinct visual graphs must be kept strictly separated rather than conflated:

2.1 行 (xíng) — The Empty Four-Stroke Crossroads

  • Visual Structure: Consists solely of the four outer strokes (彳 on the left + 亍 on the right) with an empty central channel and no person in the middle:
    • Left Wing (彳): Two curved lines intersecting at right angles—matching the brush-stroke geometry of Chu 又 (Not-Void / 90∘90^\circ90∘ geodesic crossing).
    • Right Wing (亍): A sideways T (⊢) nested on a curve—matching the brush-stroke geometry of Chu 亡 (Void / nested child origin).
    • Central Channel: The inner strokes curve toward each other at the waist and flare apart like conjugate branches of a hyperbola ()() around an open channel.
  • RSM Role: Diagrams the open four-way intersection / circulation channel itself (周行) before a local observer is indexed inside it.

2.2 Glyph 1 for 道 (衜 = 彳 + 人/首 + 亍) — The Person Inside the Four-Stroke Intersection

  • Attestation: Guo. Lao Jia (Slips A.6.19 以道佐人主, A.10.17 保此道者, A.13.3 道恆亡爲; 39017 道 sheet Row 11 cols 6–7, Row 12 col 1).
  • Visual Structure: Takes the four-stroke hyperbolic intersection (行 = 彳 + 亍) and places 人 (a person / observer head 首) right in the middle of the intersection between the four strokes.
  • RSM Role (恆道 Vantage — “Everywhere You Stand Is an Intersection”): Shows the individual observer or local node (人, indexing frame RnR_nRn​) standing right at the (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) center where the conjugate gradients of reality (彳 [又] and 亍 [亡]) meet. Every point in reality is experienced from the inside as a person standing at the crossroads of the rest of reality (Pn::On+1P_n :: O_{n+1}Pn​::On+1​).

2.3 Glyph 2 for 道 — The Water-Carrier on the Mountain Switchback

  • Attestation: Guo. Lao Jia (Rows 1–2, e.g., Slip A.39.8 天之道), Lao Yi, Lao Bing, Taiyi Shengshui.
  • Visual Structure: Depicts a complete kinesthetic engineering scene in profile:
    • Left (彳): A person striding forward with their rear foot moving off-frame to the left.
    • Top (一): A split green bamboo carrying pole (扁擔) resting across the carrier’s shoulder.
    • Upper-Middle (八): Two suspension ropes tied to the pole, angling down to hold the load.
    • Center (目/自): A rounded water bucket with two interior contour dashes showing its 3D cylindrical curvature (with the conjugate rear bucket and pole half off-frame).
    • Bottom (辶/止): The zigzag mountain switchback trail (Z-shaped hairpin path) the carrier is walking down.
  • RSM Role (可道 Vantage — Navigating a Steep Gradient via Switchback & Wobble): You cannot walk straight down a vertical mountain cliff (Bn:x=yB_n: x = yBn​:x=y) without crashing and spilling the water. Instead, the carrier traverses the gradient by alternating diagonally left (Gn+G_n^+Gn+​) and right (Gn−G_n^-Gn−​) in switchbacks, while letting the bamboo pole flex hyperbolically out of phase with their steps so their shoulder wobbles around a center of balance (PnP_nPn​) it never freezes upon.
  • Testable Bundle A Distribution ([TEXT; OPEN]): In Bundle A, when the text names the overarching invariant structure (強字之曰道 at A.21.28; 道之動 at A.37.8), the scribe draws the bilateral intersection with the person centered inside (彳 + 人/首 + 亍); when tracing a specific directional path (天之道 at A.39.8), the right-hand wing (亍) compresses into the single-wing switchback stride.

3. The Master Chain of Chapters 1, 2, 6, 25, 40, and 42

Following the Mawangdui Chapter 1 syllabus alongside Guodian Chapters 2, 25, and 40 compiles the entire model in strict deductive order:

3.1 Unrestricted Reality (道大 :: \mathcal{R}) and the Generative Paradox (玄牝 :: P_0)

  • 道大 ([G] Slip A.22.20, Ch. 25) :: \mathcal{R} (Unrestricted Reality): The graph 大 draws a standing human figure with arms and legs stretched wide open without a bounding bar. When capped above the head by a horizontal boundary bar (一), 大 becomes 天 (the bounded sky ceiling); uncapped (道大), the arms and legs stretch out forever (“no final outside, no final inside”).
  • 玄牝 (Ch. 6) :: P_0 (The Generative Paradox — Excluded Mutual Cancellation D=I\mathsf{D} = \mathsf{I}D=I):

    • Why 玄牝 :: P_0: When you follow local paradox (玄, PnP_nPn​) down into paradox infinitely (Pn↦On+1→Pn+1…P_n \mapsto O_{n+1} \to P_{n+1} \dotsPn​↦On+1​→Pn+1​…) and never hit a bottom floor, that bottomless fact—the same paradox at total scope, where Distinction and Indistinction as such would coincide (D=I\mathsf{D} = \mathsf{I}D=I)—is the Generative Paradox (玄牝, P0P_0P0​). Chapter 1 gives the two directions from which it is approached (§3.4).
    • 牝 Is Generative: 玄 is the paradox; 牝 is generative the way a womb is generative, not passive, receptive, or weak. 玄牝 is the Generative Paradox.
    • 綿綿若存 (“Unbroken, as if it existed”): Why does the text say 若存 (“as if it existed”) instead of saying it does exist (存/有)? Because P0P_0P0​ obtains as reference and cannot obtain within any frame of Reality: it is present only as the unreachable limit-reference.
    • 用之不勤 (“Drawn upon without effort”) & 道法自然 (Ch. 25, Tathātā): Because an impossibility requires zero energy and zero effort to remain unreached, under Premise 1 Reality needs no external Prime Mover to keep from collapsing into P0P_0P0​. 4.3 The Six Guodian Hydraulic & Water Anchors (Dujiangyan Mechanics)

Water in the Guodian corpus is not a moralizing sermon on political meekness; it is a physical fluid-dynamics laboratory matching the 256 BCE Dujiangyan irrigation system:

  1. 谷 (gǔ, Valley — written 浴 in Bundle A) & 浴 (yù, Basin/Valley — Slips A.2.27, A.3.4): Drawn as a double diverging split (八/八) above an open bottleneck weir mouth (口)—matching Dujiangyan’s Yuzui (splitting flow without a dam wall) and Baopingkou (metering through a rock throat).
  2. 江海所以能為百浴王，以其善下之 (Slips A.2–3, Ch. 66): Rivers and seas act as catchment kings of the hundred valleys because they occupy the lower potential energy trough (善下之 — unforced gradient descent). (Phonophore check: 工 in 江 and 每/母 in 海 function here as Old Chinese phonophores, not semantic proofs.)
  3. 濁而靜之，徐清 (Slips A.9–10, Ch. 15): Muddy, turbulent water (濁) is not cleared by stirring or straining it with hands (為之者敗之, Slip A.10.23), but by letting rotational settling (靜之 / 浧 on Slip A.16.17, 水 + settling vessel 口/皿) centrifuge and precipitate heavy sediment (天坨) out slowly (徐清)—matching Dujiangyan’s curved Feishayan spillway.
  4. 溺 (nì / ruò — Slips A.8.13, A.37.11; Ch. 78 天下莫柔弱於水 [M/R]): Written with water (水) alongside the flexing bow (弱), demonstrating how unforced hydraulic compliance outperforms rigid stone (攻堅強者莫之能勝).
  5. 上善如水 (Ch. 8): Water settles cleanly into the places ordinary men disdain (處眾人之所惡), filling the local operational void (0n0_n0n​) to establish level equilibrium.
  6. 水反輔太一 (Taiyi Shengshui Slips 1–2): Water turns back orthogonally (反) to assist 太一, condensing the reversible vapor-liquid phase cycle that forms the sky and earth frames.
