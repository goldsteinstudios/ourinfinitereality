道德經 第1章

道可道

The Opening

v2.0 •November 2026

Chapter 1 is not on the Guodian bamboo slips, the oldest copy of the text we have. The earliest witness for it is the pair of silk manuscripts from Mawangdui, from a tomb closed in 168 BCE. So this translation follows the Mawangdui wording, and shows the received text beside it where the two differ.

The full working is in the Laozi correspondence, §1.1 and §§3.1–3.5.

The text

Mawangdui

道可道也，非恆道也； 名可名也，非恆名也。 無名，萬物之始也； 有名，萬物之母也。 故恆無欲也，以觀其眇； 恆有欲也，以觀其所噭。 兩者同出，異名同謂。 玄之又玄，眾眇之門。

The silk writes 胃 for 謂. In the last line manuscript A reads 玄之有玄 and manuscript B 玄之又玄; B’s reading is shown. These are quoted from the standard transcriptions and have not yet been checked against the facsimiles.

Received

道可道，非常道；名可名，非常名。 無名天地之始；有名萬物之母。 故常無欲以觀其妙；常有欲以觀其徼。 此兩者同出而異名，同謂之玄。 玄之又玄，眾妙之門。

Three differences matter below. Mawangdui has 恆 where the received text has 常. In Mawangdui both the third and fourth lines are about 萬物, the ten thousand things, while the received text changes the first of them to 天地. And Mawangdui ends its seventh line at 同謂 and starts the next with 玄, where the received text runs them together as 同謂之玄.

Translation

道可道也，非恆道也 A way that can be laid out is not the constant way.

名可名也，非恆名也 A name that can be given is not the constant name.

無名，萬物之始也 No-name is the beginning of the ten thousand things.

有名，萬物之母也 Has-name is the mother of the ten thousand things.

故恆無欲也，以觀其眇 So turn toward the constant no, to see the fine pattern.

恆有欲也，以觀其所噭 Turn toward the constant thing, to see the edge.

兩者同出，異名同謂 The two come out together. Different names, one referent.

玄之又玄，眾眇之門 Paradox, and again paradox: the gate of all the fine patterns.

Reading it

Lines 1–2: what can be laid out, and what holds

可 marks anything laid out inside a frame: drawn, measured, defined, said. 恆 marks what holds across every frame. The two lines say these are not the same thing.

“Not” needs care. On the Guodian slips 非 is drawn as two wings curving away from each other on either side of one seam. It is a picture of two things diverging from something they share, not a picture of one thing being crossed out. A polygon drawn inside a circle is not a false circle. It is a true polygon, and no number of sides turns it into the circle. A way that can be laid out stands to the constant way like that.

The second line says the same of naming. To name something is to make a cut inside a frame: this, as against not-this. In the model’s notation that is 0ₙ ≠ 1ₙ, a distinction with a frame index on it. A constant name would be distinction with no index at all, and no name reaches that, because naming is indexing.

Lines 3–4: no-name and has-name

Split the word nothing down the middle and you get its two halves, the no and the thing. They can stand in two relations. Told apart: tree, as against not-tree. Or not told apart.

  • 無名, no-name :: the no and the thing of a frame not told apart (0ₙ = 1ₙ).
  • 有名, has-name :: the thing told apart from its no (0ₙ ≠ 1ₙ).

The lines then say where each sits.

  • No-name is the 始, the beginning :: the frame’s origin, Oₙ, the corner where its two axes meet. Nothing of the frame is told apart there.
  • Has-name is the 母, the mother :: the frame’s local paradox, Pₙ, where the balance line crosses the curve. It is the balance of the named frame: it must obtain as reference, and it cannot obtain within the frame. The next frame begins from it.

So the mother of one frame is the beginning of the next.

Notice that in Mawangdui both lines are about the same ten thousand things. The same things are begun in one relation and mothered by the other. You don’t get distinction without the indistinction it is measured from.

Reading 無名 and 有名 as units, and not as “無, the name of…”, follows the oldest witness. Chapter 1 is missing from the Guodian slips, but both compounds are on them: 亡名 at slips A.18.12–13 and A.13.27–28, and 又名 at A.19.28–29, directly after the graph for cutting.

Lines 5–6: two directions

On the Guodian slips 欲 is written with 谷, a valley channel, and 欠, opening toward. It reads as turning your channel toward something more than as desire.

The two lines turn it in two directions. Toward the constant no, what shows is 眇, the fine pattern. Toward the constant thing, what shows is 所噭, the edge, where things stop.

The constant no and the constant thing are the halves taken all the way: pure no with no thing, and pure thing with no no. Neither is ever reached. They are two directions of approach to one paradox, not two places to arrive.

Line 7: the two come out together

The two are no-name and has-name. They come out together because neither comes without the other. They have different names and one referent.

That holds inside one frame, and it holds across two. The place a parent frame calls its mother is the place the child frame calls its beginning. Different names, one referent.

Mawangdui stops the sentence there, and only then says 玄. The text states the thing first and names it second.

Line 8: paradox, and again paradox

玄 is the name for what the last line stated: what must obtain as reference and cannot obtain within the frame. On the Guodian slips it is drawn as two loops twisted into a figure-eight around a pinch at the crossing.

玄之又玄 is not one paradox inside another. It is paradox, and again paradox. Picture following a leaf vein through the bud node, down the twig pith to the twig node, down the branch pith to the trunk, and down the trunk pith to the seed the tree started from, which is no longer there. Each node is the beginning of what grows from it. That is the gate the patterns come through.

Confidence Assessment

無名 and 有名 read as units plausible

The other parse puts a comma after 無 and 有 and makes 名 a verb. This page follows the unit parse because the Guodian slips already write both compounds: 亡名 and 又名. Open question O04 in the chains.

恆無 and 恆有 as what 欲 is turned toward plausible

In the Mawangdui text 也 comes after 欲, which also allows 恆無欲也 as one unit: 'constantly without desire'. The parse is open (O04).

The Mawangdui wording of the last two lines plausible

Quoted from the standard transcriptions. Manuscript A reads 玄之有玄 and manuscript B 玄之又玄. Not yet checked against the facsimiles.
